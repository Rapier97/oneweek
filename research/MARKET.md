# Oneweek market research

Written 28 October 2026. Desk research from web sources; most figures come from vendor surveys with small samples, so treat them as direction rather than precision. Oneweek's offer for reference: app, website or automation built in a week for 2,000 pounds, optional 200 pounds a month care plan.

## 1. Who pays 1.5k to 3k for a fast build

Scored 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). Need is how badly the admin pain hurts today. Reach is how easy a solo founder with no audience can get in front of them. Money is whether a 2k invoice is a normal decision for the owner.

| Rank | Niche | Need | Reach | Money | The one pain they most want gone | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Small domiciliary care agencies (5 to 40 carers) | 5 | 3 | 4 | The weekly rota: matching carers to visits by hours, travel and training, done in a spreadsheet and phone calls, redone every time someone is sick | New homecare registrations in England grew 11% in 2024/25, mostly small providers. Vendors describe spreadsheets plus phone calls as the baseline. Birdie and ShiftCare charge from 7 pounds per carer per month, so owners already pay for software but off-the-shelf tools are built for bigger agencies |
| 2 | Trades with 2 to 10 staff (heating, electrical, building) | 5 | 4 | 4 | Quotes, invoices and chasing payment done in the evening. The job sheet to invoice gap | Powered Now: 5h20 a week on quotes, invoices and chasing; separate survey says 7 hours. HeyBRB: 8 hours a week, 77% of trades do admin in the evening, nearly half at weekends. They already spend 100 to 300 pounds a month on Checkatrade, so money moves |
| 3 | Outdoor activity, climbing and leisure centres | 4 | 3 | 3 | Instructor rota by qualification and availability, currently Excel plus a staffing admin | Oneweek's first client. Climbing Lab (Leeds) called Excel rotas time consuming and hard to keep staff informed. Kingswood job ad shows spreadsheets plus separate software still normal in 2025. Only one specialist tool (Cinolla) in the market |
| 4 | Independent gyms, studios and PTs | 4 | 4 | 3 | Class bookings and waitlists run through WhatsApp groups and a shared spreadsheet; no-shows and chasing memberships | Vendors describe WhatsApp groups plus a shared spreadsheet as the standard start. Crowded with SaaS (TeamUp, Gymcatch, ClubRight) so the win is the odd case the SaaS does not fit: hybrid memberships, bespoke pricing, kids' clubs |
| 5 | Holiday let owners and small letting agencies (3 to 15 properties) | 3 | 3 | 4 | Changeover day: cleaner and linen scheduling on a printed sheet and a WhatsApp group, which breaks at about 12 properties | About 212,500 holiday lets in England; average owner gross income 25,600 pounds in 2025. One agency (Anglesey) ran several spreadsheets updated weekly, monthly and quarterly. Owners have cash but are seasonal and cautious after tax changes |
| 6 | Small recruitment agencies (candidate led, 1 to 5 recruiters) | 4 | 3 | 5 | Tracking which candidate was sent to which client, because vacancy-led ATS tools do not fit candidate-led work, so they fall back to a spreadsheet | UK recruiter on Zoho forum went back to spreadsheets for this reason. Vendors quote 1,500 to 3,000 pounds for CRM setup, so the price is already normal. High margins per placement |
| 7 | Salons, barbers and clinics | 3 | 4 | 2 | No-shows and late cancellations, about 7% of monthly revenue self-reported | Fresha survey Dec 2025, 200 UK businesses. But Fresha, Treatwell and Booksy are free or cheap and good, so a bespoke build rarely beats them. Low ticket, cash tight |
| 8 | Sports and community clubs (volunteer treasurer) | 4 | 4 | 1 | Chasing subs and membership renewals by email, reconciling by hand | Real pain and easy to reach through local Facebook, but it is a volunteer spending club money. 2k is a committee vote. Good for referrals, poor for revenue |

Also considered and parked: marketing agencies (reporting pain is real but they buy SaaS like AgencyAnalytics and are suspicious of other agencies), driving schools and tutors (cheap SaaS already covers bookings), dog groomers (same).

Nobody found is selling "the spreadsheet you run the business on, turned into an app" to a named niche at a fixed weekly price. That gap is Oneweek's position.

Verdict: 2,000 a week is cheap for the work and fair for the buyer. It is below the freelance developer median, half of the nearest design studio, and a third below the only direct app competitor. For the buyer it is the same price as a brochure website from a freelancer, which is the number small business owners already have in their heads. There is room to go to 2,500 once there are three case studies. 200 a month is at the top of the standard care plan band, which is right because it covers an app with a database, not a brochure site. Say what it includes in hours (for example up to 3 hours of changes a month) so it does not read as dear next to a 50 pound WordPress plan.

## 4. Where to find these owners

Ranked for a solo founder with no audience, weighting cost, time to first conversation and fit with the top niches.

| Rank | Channel | Why | How to work it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Referrals from client one and their suppliers | 78% of UK small business owners rate a referral from another owner as important when buying. The Portsmouth client knows other activity providers, their accountant, their insurer | Ask for three names at handover. Offer a one-month care plan credit per introduction |
| 2 | Local Facebook groups (town business groups, "Portsmouth tradesmen", care manager groups) | 70% of UK adults are on Facebook; small business staff are only 14% of LinkedIn members. Trades and care managers live here, not on LinkedIn | Answer admin questions for free, no links. One short post a week showing a before (spreadsheet) and after (app) |
| 3 | Care and trade associations and their WhatsApp groups | Homecare Association regional meetings, local Care Provider Alliances, merchant counter WhatsApp groups for trades | Get invited by one member. Offer a free 20-minute "what should stay in the spreadsheet" call |
| 4 | Local networking (BNI, chamber, 4Networking) | BNI is 700 to 800 pounds a year plus weekly fees. Slow but it fills a referral chair with accountants and insurers who see owners' admin | Visit as a guest for two months before paying. Chamber events are cheaper and looser |
| 5 | Reddit (r/smallbusinessuk, r/UKPersonalFinance for landlords) and UK Business Forums | Good for watching the words buyers use, poor for direct selling. Searches for custom app quotes turned up vendors, not owners | Read weekly, reply to admin and quote threads, no pitching |

Not ranked: Checkatrade and Bark are consumer directories, so Oneweek cannot list there, but their members are the trades niche and their 100 to 300 pound monthly spend is proof they pay for leads. LinkedIn is worth a profile for recruitment agencies and care agency directors only.

## 5. Messaging

Phrases buyers actually use, from surveys and forum threads:

1. "I do the admin in the evenings" (77% of trades, HeyBRB) or "I do my invoicing on Sunday".
2. "It's all on a spreadsheet" or "we run it off a spreadsheet and WhatsApp".
3. "I've looked at the software but it doesn't do what we do" (candidate-led recruiters, activity centres, hybrid gyms).

Recommended one-line pitch: "I turn the spreadsheet you run your business on into an app, in a week, for 2,000 pounds."

Headline options for the site:

1. Your spreadsheet, turned into an app. Built in a week.
2. The admin you do on Sunday night, gone by Friday.
3. Small business software that does what you do. One week, 2,000 pounds.

Keep "what is not included" and a Monday to Friday timetable on the page, as App in a Week does. Buyers in this bracket trust a fixed list more than a portfolio.

## 6. Risks and how others scope around them

| What goes wrong | Why | How others handle it |
|---|---|---|
| Scope creeps in small pieces | "While you're in there" requests, 30 in one case cited | A written "not building" list signed before Monday. Changes after kick-off are priced, not debated |
| Client is not available during the week | One-week builds need same-day answers | App in a Week sends a pre-project pack and needs it back 48 hours before Monday; daily build shared |
| The hard part was missed at scoping | Integrations, imports of messy spreadsheet data, payments | Free 30-minute scoping call, then a one-page spec. Anything regulated, real-time or multi-party payment is excluded |
| Old data migration eats the week | Years of spreadsheet rows with inconsistent columns | Import only the current period. Charge migration separately or do it in month one of the care plan |
| Client wants a design agency finish | Expectation gap at 2k | Basic design system, their logo and colours, no custom illustration. Say so on the site |
| App store review and third-party delays | Outside the builder's control | Build web apps by default; native only when there is a clear need. Rejections resubmitted free if the builder's fault |
| Payment risk on a one-week job | No time to chase | 50% deposit on booking, balance before kick-off Monday, as App in a Week does. Oneweek's quote template should follow this |
| No maintenance means a dead app in six months | Competitor excludes it | The 200 pound care plan is the differentiator. Sell it as the default, not the add-on |

## Recommendation

Go after small domiciliary care agencies and trades with 2 to 10 staff first. Care agencies have the sharpest pain (the rota), a regulator that pushes them towards tidy records, a growing number of small new entrants, and they already pay per-carer software fees, so a 2k build plus 200 a month is a familiar shape. The Portsmouth rota app is a direct case study for them. Trades are the easiest to reach in volume through local Facebook groups and merchant WhatsApp groups, have measurable pain (5 to 8 hours a week of evening admin) and already spend 100 to 300 pounds a month with Checkatrade, so the money is proven. Keep outdoor and leisure centres as the warm referral niche from client one rather than a cold target, because there are few of them per town. Leave salons, clubs and agencies for inbound only.
