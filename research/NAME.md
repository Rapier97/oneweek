## What the evidence says

- **Names that state the benefit help people remember that benefit.** In a Journal of Marketing study, a name that spelled out a product benefit made people better at recalling an advert claim about that benefit, and worse at recalling unrelated claims. Such names are also harder to reposition later. https://core.ac.uk/works/16484138 , https://www.msi.org/working-papers/the-effects-of-brand-name-suggestiveness-on-advertising-recall/
- **Names that are easy to say earn more trust.** Easy-to-pronounce stock names drew more buyers (Alter and Oppenheimer, PNAS 2006), and eBay sellers with short, easy names were rated more trustworthy. https://phys.org/news/2006-06-company-stock.pdf , https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.02200/full
- **Repeated sounds win when a name is said aloud.** People chose names with repeated sounds more often, and the effect was strongest when the name was spoken (Argo et al., Journal of Marketing 2010). https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/10/101018092237.htm
- **Firms named after their founder earn about 3 points more return on assets** across 1.8 million European firms. The authors read this as a sign of the founder's confidence, not something the name causes. https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/chatterji-eponymy
- **Word of mouth is the main way small service firms win work** (64% of UK freelance copywriters, and the top channel in a survey of 200+ UK SMEs). So the name has to survive being repeated by someone else. https://statista.com/statistics/972358/freelance-copywriters-finding-new-clients-in-the-uk , https://smallbusiness.co.uk/word-of-mouth-most-powerful-marketing-tool-1623258/
- **Speed can lower perceived value.** People rate work higher when it looks like it took more effort (Kruger 2004), and showing the work raises perceived value (Buell and Norton 2011). https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effort_heuristic , https://www.marketingweek.com/richard-shotton-labour-illusion/
- **No study tests "X in a week" names directly.** Similar one-week offers sell at $3,900 to $6,000 (about £3,000 to £4,500), so the format does not force a low price. A web search found no studio already trading as "Done in a Week". https://contra.com/s/OF6P2m2X-website-in-a-week

## Why Done in a Week

Alex wins work by talking to people and through referrals, and the research shows a name that states its benefit is the one people remember and pass on, so "Done in a Week" carries the whole pitch every time someone repeats it. It is easy to say, has no rival using it, has both the .com and .co.uk free, and is already on the site, terms and kit, so switching would cost time for no proven gain. The one risk, that speed can make the work look cheap, is handled by the price and by showing the work, while Alex's own name still does the trust job in person ("I'm Alex, I run Done in a Week").
